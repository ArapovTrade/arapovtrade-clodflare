1. First steps in trading

Trading for beginners

What is trading?

Trading means buying and selling assets, such as shares or currencies, to profit from price changes. A trade can make money or lose it.

Read the full explanation

Learning to trade

Is trading right for everyone?

Trading takes study, patience and the ability to follow rules through losses. It may not suit someone who expects quick money without risk.

Read the full explanation

Long vs short

What do long and short mean?

Going long means buying with the expectation that the price will rise. A traditional short involves borrowing an asset, selling it and buying it back later, hoping the price falls.

Read the full explanation

Price and volume

Why look at price levels and volume together?

Levels show areas where price has stopped or turned before. Volume shows trading activity. Igor Arapov uses both to assess what is happening in the market.

Read the full explanation

Choosing a market

What should a beginner trade first?

Igor Arapov suggests learning one actively traded instrument first. Following it closely can make its behaviour easier to understand than watching many markets at once.

Read the full explanation

How to start trading

Where should I start learning to trade?

Learn how the market works, choose a method and practise on a demo account. Review a series of trades before moving to a small live account with clear risk limits.

Read the full explanation

Trading account balance

How much money do I need to start trading?

It depends on the market, broker and size of your trades. The important question is how much you could lose, rather than how much you hope to earn.

Read the full explanation

Entry, stop and target

What should I decide before opening a trade?

Decide why you are entering, where you will exit if wrong and how much you can lose. Also choose a profit target before opening the trade.

Read the full explanation

Demo account

What can I learn on a demo account?

A demo account uses virtual money, so you can learn the platform and test rules without risking your funds. Live trading adds the emotions of real gains and losses.

Read the full explanation

Beginner trading mistakes

What mistakes can wipe out a trading account?

Large positions, trading without a stop and trying to win losses back can quickly damage an account. A good entry does not protect you from taking too much risk.

Read the full explanation

Trading education

Can I learn trading on my own?

You can study the basics, choose a method and practise with a trading journal. A clear learning order is more useful than constantly switching between unrelated tips.

Read the full explanation

Trading discipline

What habits should a beginner develop?

Follow your plan, limit losses and review your trades. In Arapov's approach, consistently following simple rules matters more than finding a secret signal.

Read the full explanation

2. Trading and long-term investing

Trading time horizon

How is trading different from investing?

Traders usually aim to profit from shorter price moves. Investors generally hold assets longer, seeking growth or income from ownership.

Read the full explanation

Time spent trading

Does trading take more time than investing?

Active trading usually requires more frequent decisions about entries and exits. Long-term investing generally involves fewer trades and a longer holding period.

Read the full explanation

Trading returns

Is trading more profitable than investing?

There is no single answer. Results depend on skill, risk, costs and the method used. Making more trades does not automatically mean earning more.

Read the full explanation

Protecting your money

How do traders and investors manage risk?

Traders can limit each trade with position sizing and exit rules. Investors may spread their money across assets and use a longer time horizon. Both need a plan for losses.

Read the full explanation

Choosing an approach

Should I trade or invest?

Consider your goals, available time and ability to handle losses. The approach should fit your life, rather than promises of unusually high returns.

Read the full explanation

3. Checking your results

Profit factor vs win rate

Can a high win rate still lose money?

Yes. Win rate counts how often trades win; profit factor compares total gains with total losses. Many small wins can be outweighed by a few large losses and trading costs.

Read the full explanation

4. Brokers and trading software

Trading platform

What is a trading platform?

It is software for viewing markets and placing or managing trades. The available features depend on the platform and connected broker.

Read the full explanation

TradingView for beginners

What can I do with TradingView?

You can view charts, mark levels and use indicators. It also offers paper trading so you can practise with virtual money.

Read the full explanation

TradingView volume

How does Arapov set up his chart?

He keeps the chart simple, using price bars, levels and volume from exchange-traded futures. This makes price movement and trading activity easier to compare.

Read the full explanation

Paper trading practice

How should I practise with virtual money?

Use written rules and record a series of trades. Review both the results and whether you followed the plan before considering live trading.

Read the full explanation

Broker vs exchange

How is a broker different from an exchange?

An exchange organises trading. A broker gives clients access to markets, while a platform is the software they use. These are different roles.

Read the full explanation

Choosing a broker

How do I choose a broker?

Check regulation, fees, available markets and withdrawal terms. Find out how client funds are held and who handles complaints.

Read the full explanation

Broker warning signs

How can I spot a suspicious broker?

Be wary of guaranteed returns, pressure to deposit and unclear withdrawal rules. Check the company through official sources rather than relying on its advertising.

Read the full explanation

Price alerts

What is a price alert?

It notifies you when price reaches a level or another chosen condition occurs. An alert helps you monitor a market, but it is not a reason to trade on its own.

Read the full explanation

5. Choosing a trading style

Types of trading

What are scalping, day trading and swing trading?

Scalping uses very short trades. Day trading opens and closes trades within a day. Swing trading holds positions for several days or longer.

Read the full explanation

Scalping fees

Why do fees matter so much in scalping?

Scalpers make many trades for small price moves. Commissions and other costs can therefore take a large share of their gains.

Read the full explanation

Day trading

What is day trading?

Day trading means opening and closing positions during the same trading day. It avoids holding positions overnight but requires frequent attention and decisions.

Read the full explanation

Swing trading

What is swing trading?

Swing trading aims to capture moves over several days or weeks. It involves less frequent trading, but positions can be affected by overnight news and price gaps.

Read the full explanation

Position vs algorithmic trading

What is the difference between position trading and algorithmic trading?

Position trading describes holding a trade for a long time. Algorithmic trading means software follows trading rules. A strategy can be both.

Read the full explanation

Trading costs

Why can frequent trading be expensive?

Each trade may involve a spread, commission or other cost. More trades increase total costs, especially when each expected gain is small.

Read the full explanation

Trading pace

Which trading style does Arapov suggest for beginners?

He favours a slower approach that gives time to assess trades and follow rules. Fast scalping can be harder while a beginner is still developing discipline.

Read the full explanation

High-frequency trading

Why does speed matter in HFT?

HFT uses computers to place trades extremely quickly. Being closer to an exchange can reduce the time it takes to send orders, which matters when competing over tiny price moves.

Read the full explanation

6. Trading with a prop firm

Prop trading

What is prop trading?

A prop firm offers trading access under its own rules and shares results with the trader. Check how the programme, account and fees actually work before joining.

Read the full explanation

Prop challenge limits

What can make me fail a prop challenge?

Breaking a daily or overall loss limit can end the challenge, even if other trades were profitable. Read the rules and plan your risk before trading.

Read the full explanation

Funded account

What is a funded trading account?

It is an account made available after you meet a prop firm's conditions. Profit-sharing and loss limits continue to apply after you receive access.

Read the full explanation

Personal vs prop account

Should a beginner use a prop firm?

Arapov recommends first developing a method and learning to control losses. A prop programme adds fees and strict rules; it does not replace trading skills.

Read the full explanation

7. Following another trader

Copy trading

What is copy trading?

It automatically copies another trader's trades into your account. You can share in the results, but you also take the risk of their decisions.

Read the full explanation

Copy trading positions

How are trades copied to my account?

A platform reproduces a selected trader's positions according to its settings. Check how trade sizes are matched and what controls you have over copying.

Read the full explanation

Learning from copied trades

Can copy trading help me learn?

It can show how someone sizes trades and handles losses if you actively study their decisions. Copying alone does not teach you how to build your own method.

Read the full explanation

Copy trading rankings

Is the top-ranked trader the safest choice?

No. High past returns can come from high risk. Look at losses, position sizes and the length of the record, rather than choosing by returns alone.

Read the full explanation

8. Using AI as an assistant

AI trading tools

How can AI help a trader?

AI can organise information, examine trading records and suggest possible explanations. You still need to check its output and take responsibility for decisions and risk.

Read the full explanation

AI price predictions

Can AI predict market prices accurately every time?

No. Models use incomplete past data, while news and market behaviour change. A confident prediction does not guarantee the next price move.

Read the full explanation

Trading journal analysis

Can AI help review my trades?

It can help find patterns in your records, such as repeated mistakes or times when you lose more often. That reviews past trades rather than guaranteeing future prices.

Read the full explanation

AI and trading rules

Can AI replace a trading system?

It does not replace clear entry rules, exit rules and risk limits. Even an automated approach needs testing and oversight.

Read the full explanation

Trading probabilities

Can I trade without predicting every price move?

Yes. A system can work with probabilities and a planned exit when a trade fails. The aim is a sound result across many trades, rather than being right every time.

Read the full explanation

9. Building consistent trading rules

Trading system

What is a trading system?

It is a set of rules for entering, exiting and managing risk. Written rules help you make consistent decisions and review your results.

Read the full explanation

Trading system components

What rules does a trading system need?

It needs entry conditions, exits, trade size and a loss limit. You also need a way to check results and whether you followed those rules.

Read the full explanation

Trading plan

How is a trading plan different from a system?

A system describes the method. A trading plan sets out how you will use it, including markets, entries, exits, risk and when to stop trading.

Read the full explanation

Backtesting

What is backtesting?

Backtesting checks trading rules against past data. If rules only work on the data used to tune them, they may have been fitted to history rather than finding a reliable pattern.

Read the full explanation

Forward testing

What is forward testing?

It checks ready-made rules on new trades as they occur, often on a demo account. Record results and whether the rules are practical to follow.

Read the full explanation

Trading journal

What should I write in a trading journal?

Record the instrument, reason for entry, exit, risk and result. Include mistakes and emotions so you can see what needs improvement.

Read the full explanation

Mechanical vs discretionary trading

What is the difference between mechanical and discretionary trading?

A mechanical system has explicit rules for each action. Discretionary trading allows judgement about the situation, but still needs rules and risk limits.

Read the full explanation
