1. Understanding indicator signals

Trading indicators

What is a trading indicator?

It is a calculation based on market data, usually price or volume.

Read the full explanation

Indicator crossovers

Is an indicator crossover enough to trade?

Arapov does not use a crossover alone as an entry. He also looks at levels and volume because crossovers can repeat without a lasting price move.

Read the full explanation

Indicator divergence

What is divergence in trading?

Divergence means price and an indicator are not making matching highs or lows. It can suggest that the price movement is losing strength, but it is not a complete entry signal.

Read the full explanation

Divergence confirmation

Does divergence guarantee a reversal?

No. A strong trend can continue through several divergences. Define what price response would confirm the idea and what would invalidate it; a disagreement alone is not confirmation that the market has turned.

Read the full explanation

Indicators in chart reading

Which indicators does Arapov find useful?

Trading volume is part of Arapov’s core chart reading. He gives calculated indicators a supporting role when they add a clear observation; the course does not establish a separate RSI or MACD entry system.

2. Measuring trend strength

ADX indicator

What does ADX measure?

ADX measures trend strength, not direction. A high reading does not tell you whether price is moving up or down.

Read the full explanation

ADX trend strength

What does a high or low ADX mean?

Below 20 and above 25 are commonly used guides to weaker and stronger directional movement, not exact market boundaries. A high reading does not choose direction or prove that a move has ended.

Read the full explanation

ADX directional lines

What are +DI and −DI?

These lines describe upward and downward directional movement. ADX measures the strength of that movement, so the lines serve different purposes.

Read the full explanation

ADX trend filter

Can ADX help distinguish a trend from a range?

It can help describe whether movement is strongly directional or weak. That can guide the choice of approach, but does not provide a trade by itself.

Read the full explanation

ADX trade direction

Does a high ADX mean I should buy?

No. ADX measures directional strength without choosing its side. It can rise while price falls. Read direction separately from +DI and −DI and from price highs and lows.

Read the full explanation

ADX settings

What does the ADX period change?

It changes how much recent data is used and how quickly the indicator reacts. A 14-period setting is common, but changing it does not remove reliance on past movement.

Read the full explanation

3. Measuring price fluctuations

ATR volatility

What does ATR measure?

ATR averages the size of price fluctuations over a chosen period, including gaps. It can rise during either an upward or downward move.

Read the full explanation

ATR stop-loss

How can ATR help choose a stop distance?

First define the price change that would invalidate the trade, then compare the exit distance with recent ATR. Before entry, a wider necessary distance calls for a smaller position if the loss budget is unchanged. No ATR multiplier guarantees that ordinary movement will not trigger a stop.

Read the full explanation

ATR and position size

Why adjust position size when an ATR stop gets wider?

Before entry, a wider necessary stop distance calls for a smaller quantity to fit the same money risk. Rising ATR after entry does not authorize moving the stop farther away or increasing the original loss budget.

Read the full explanation

High ATR

What can an unusually high ATR mean?

It means recent price ranges are large. Arapov treats an extreme reading as a reason to review the trade and its exit, rather than an automatic reversal signal.

Read the full explanation

Low ATR

Does low ATR tell me which way price will break?

No. Low ATR describes smaller recent true ranges, not the direction of the next breakout. Check how price behaves after crossing the boundary; ATR does not distinguish a lasting breakout from a failed one.

Read the full explanation

True Range

How does True Range account for gaps?

It compares the candle's high-low range with the distances from the previous close to the high and low. The largest distance is used.

Read the full explanation

Chandelier Exit

What is a Chandelier Exit?

Chandelier Exit calculates a line a multiple of ATR below a period high for a buying position or above a period low for a position opened to benefit from a fall. The line can move in either direction. Protective-order update and execution rules must be defined separately.

Read the full explanation

4. Comparing price averages

MACD indicator

What is MACD?

MACD compares two averages of past prices, one using a shorter period and one a longer period. Its signal line smooths that difference. These readings describe price movement rather than predict future prices.

Read the full explanation

MACD signals

What signals can MACD show?

Traders watch the MACD line cross its signal line or zero, the histogram (the difference between the MACD and signal lines), and divergence, a disagreement between price and indicator highs or lows. Each describes a different comparison, not a guaranteed entry.

Read the full explanation

MACD settings

What do 12, 26 and 9 mean in MACD?

They are the periods used for the two moving averages and the signal line. Faster settings react sooner but can produce more short-lived signals.

Read the full explanation

Conflicting indicator signals

Why can MACD and stochastic disagree?

They use price data differently, so their readings can conflict. Arapov checks the chart and volume instead of deciding by whichever indicator looks more convincing.

Read the full explanation

MACD limitations

Why does Arapov avoid trading on MACD alone?

The course puts price structure, levels and trading activity before indicators. MACD processes prices already received and cannot replace a reason for entry or a loss limit. This is the course's general supporting role for indicators, not evidence of a separate personal MACD strategy.

Read the full explanation

MACD timeframes

Why can MACD differ on hourly and daily charts?

Each chart uses a different set of price periods. A short pullback can therefore appear against a broader trend.

Read the full explanation

5. Comparing gains and losses

RSI indicator

What is RSI?

RSI compares averaged price gains and losses on a scale from 0 to 100. It measures their relative size, not the number of buyers or sellers or the probability of a reversal.

Read the full explanation

RSI overbought and oversold

Does RSI above 70 mean I should sell?

No. RSI can stay high during a strong uptrend or low during a downtrend. Check the market situation rather than treating 70 and 30 as automatic commands.

Read the full explanation

RSI divergence

What is RSI divergence?

RSI divergence occurs when corresponding price and RSI extremes disagree: a lower price low with a higher RSI low, or a higher price high with a lower RSI high. It does not set a reversal time and needs a separate price condition before a trade.

Read the full explanation

RSI signals

Can RSI tell me exactly when to enter?

No. RSI processes price changes already received. It does not reveal future news or orders. The course treats indicators as supporting tools; entry, exit and permitted loss need their own rules.

Read the full explanation

RSI settings

Which RSI settings should a beginner learn first?

Begin with ordinary RSI using a 14-period length and closing prices. Record the chart period and source, and test one fixed rule before changing the settings. A shorter length reacts faster; a longer one smooths more, but neither is universally best.

Read the full explanation

Perfect indicator settings

Is there a perfect RSI setting?

No setting turns RSI into a forecast. Fix the settings and observation time, with separate entry, exit and permitted-loss rules. Compare the same rule with and without the RSI condition, including failures and costs, rather than changing thresholds after each outcome.

Read the full explanation

RSI calculation

How is RSI calculated in simple terms?

It compares average gains with average losses over a period, then converts the ratio to a value between 0 and 100. Larger relative gains produce a higher reading.

Read the full explanation

6. Locating price within a range

Stochastic oscillator

What does the stochastic oscillator show?

It compares the closing price with the recent high-low range. Its main line, %K, and smoothed line, %D, are shown on a scale from 0 to 100.

Read the full explanation

Stochastic settings

What do 14, 3 and 3 mean?

They describe the lookback period and smoothing used by the indicator. They are common starting settings, not a formula for guaranteed trades.

Read the full explanation

Stochastic signals

Does stochastic above 80 mean price must fall?

No. A high reading describes a calculated position near the range’s upper end; smoothed readings also include earlier values. It can persist while price keeps rising. A threshold alone does not confirm a reversal.

Read the full explanation

Stochastic in trends

Why can stochastic stay overbought for a long time?

In a strong uptrend, price can keep closing near the top of its range. Signals against that trend may therefore fail repeatedly.

Read the full explanation

Too many indicators

Does adding more indicators make a trade safer?

Not necessarily. Several indicators may repeat the same price information. Arapov prefers a clear chart with levels and volume to a stack of conflicting signals.

Read the full explanation

7. Following average prices

SMA vs EMA

How is an SMA different from an EMA?

An SMA gives equal weight to prices in its period. An EMA gives more weight to recent prices, so it usually reacts faster. Both use past data.

Read the full explanation

Moving-average signals

Why do moving averages give false signals?

They react after price moves and may cross repeatedly in a range. Changing the period trades off quicker reactions against more noise.

Read the full explanation

Moving-average support

Can a moving average act as support or resistance?

Price sometimes reacts near a moving average during a trend. The line does not display pending orders or prove why the reaction happened. Define the required price response before treating it as an extra condition.

Read the full explanation

200-day moving average

What is the 200-day moving average used for?

On a daily chart, a 200-day average provides a reference for longer-term movement. Position relative to the line can be a filter condition, but it does not choose an entry or guarantee continuation.

Read the full explanation

Golden cross vs death cross

What are a golden cross and a death cross?

A golden cross is a faster moving average crossing above a slower one. A death cross is the reverse. The 50- and 200-period averages are commonly used.

Read the full explanation

8. Weighting prices by volume

VWAP indicator

What is VWAP?

VWAP is the average traded price weighted by volume. A session VWAP starts again each session and is mainly used for intraday analysis.

Read the full explanation

VWAP execution benchmark

Why do large traders compare trades with VWAP?

They can use it to assess the average price achieved while buying or selling a large amount. This makes VWAP an execution reference as well as a chart line.

Read the full explanation

Price above or below VWAP

What does price above VWAP mean?

It means the current price is above the volume-weighted average for the selected period. This comparison alone is not a buy signal; specify the price reaction being checked before testing it.

Read the full explanation

VWAP reclaim and rejection

What are a VWAP reclaim and rejection?

A reclaim is a move back across VWAP followed by holding on the other side. A rejection is an approach followed by a turn away. Define the observation period and what counts as holding before testing either condition.

Read the full explanation

Anchored VWAP

How is anchored VWAP different from session VWAP?

Anchored VWAP starts at a chosen event or chart point rather than resetting each session. It can therefore cover a longer move.

Read the full explanation

VWAP with price levels

Why combine VWAP with support and resistance?

Their overlap can provide context, but both may reflect the same price history rather than independent confirmations. Observe the subsequent reaction and test whether VWAP adds information to the price-level rule.

Read the full explanation

9. Watching changing price ranges

Bollinger Bands

What do Bollinger Bands show?

They show bands around a moving average based on how widely prices vary. Wider bands reflect greater recent variation; narrower bands reflect less.

Read the full explanation

Bollinger Band touches

Should I trade whenever price touches a band?

No. Price can follow a band during a sustained trend. A touch describes its position relative to the recent mean, not a confirmed reversal. Check the actual price movement before treating a touch as a trade condition.

Read the full explanation

Bollinger squeeze

What is a Bollinger squeeze?

It is a narrowing of the bands as price variation decreases. It flags a quieter market, but does not determine the direction of the next move.

Read the full explanation

BandWidth and Keltner Channels

How can BandWidth help spot a squeeze?

BandWidth measures how narrow the Bollinger Bands are. Comparing them with Keltner Channels can help define a squeeze, but still does not predict its direction.

Read the full explanation

Bollinger breakout

How does Arapov assess a breakout from the bands?

The course treats indicators as supporting tools after price structure, levels and trading activity. It does not establish a separate personal Bollinger-band breakout rule. Describe price movement first, then test whether band width or position adds a useful condition. Fix the settings and retain failed patterns instead of changing them after seeing the outcome.

Read the full explanation

10. Reading the cloud and its lines

Ichimoku lines

What are the Ichimoku lines based on?

The five plots use different calculations. Tenkan and Kijun are range midpoints, Spans A and B form the forward-displaced cloud, and Chikou is the current close displayed in the past. These shifts do not reveal future prices.

Read the full explanation

Ichimoku cloud

What does price above or below the cloud mean?

Compare price with both cloud boundaries on the same vertical line. Above both is conventionally an upward picture, below both a downward picture, and inside the cloud direction is less clear. That classification does not guarantee the next move.

Read the full explanation

Tenkan-Kijun crossover

What is a Tenkan-Kijun crossover?

It is a new crossing of Tenkan and Kijun, not simply one line staying above the other. A bullish crossing above the aligned cloud agrees with an upward picture; below it the conditions conflict. Check a completed bar if the rule requires one, rather than treating the crossing as an automatic trade.

Read the full explanation

Learning Ichimoku

Should a beginner start with Ichimoku?

Arapov suggests first learning to read trends and levels directly. You can study the cloud later once the basic chart makes sense.

Read the full explanation
