1. Reading trading activity

Volume analysis

What does trading volume show?

Exchange volume measures quantity traded during a period, such as shares or futures contracts, rather than the number of traders. A completed trade contributes its quantity once, although it has both a buyer and a seller.

Read the full explanation

Effort vs result

What does effort versus result mean in Wyckoff?

In Wyckoff analysis, traded quantity is the effort and price progress is the result. Large activity with limited progress can suggest opposing orders limiting movement. Check the closing position and subsequent reaction; the observation does not identify a participant or confirm a reversal.

Read the full explanation

Price-volume divergence

What does a new high on lower volume mean?

It can suggest that a price move is attracting less trading activity. Arapov treats this as a warning to examine the move, not a guaranteed reversal.

Read the full explanation

Weak demand and supply

Why can price fall without a surge in selling?

Arapov explains that reduced buying demand can allow price to fall. Similarly, limited selling supply can allow price to rise.

Read the full explanation

Volume Profile

What are POC and the value area?

POC is the price with the most traded volume in a profile. The value area covers a concentration of volume, with upper and lower boundaries called VAH and VAL.

Read the full explanation

Market Profile

How is Market Profile different from Wyckoff analysis?

Time-based Market Profile records which price areas were visited in separate time blocks; Volume Profile records traded quantity by price. Wyckoff instead examines price and activity as a developing sequence. These are different descriptions, not proof of who traded or a documented personal Market Profile rule.

Read the full explanation

Volume levels

How does Arapov choose a volume level?

In the course example, Arapov checks an area where active trading accompanied an advance that stalled. He then examines changed price structure, a return toward the earlier area and renewed movement. One volume concentration is not a complete entry condition.

Read the full explanation

Exchange vs tick volume

Is all volume data the same?

No. Exchange volume measures quantity traded in the selected market. Retail Forex tick volume counts incoming quote updates, not all global currency transactions. Futures and individual crypto exchanges also have their own coverage.

Read the full explanation

Automatic Rally

What is an Automatic Rally?

An Automatic Rally is a significant rebound after intense selling stops near a Selling Climax. In an accumulation schematic, its high helps identify the developing range’s upper area. The rebound alone does not confirm completed accumulation.

Read the full explanation

Spring vs upthrust

How is a spring different from an upthrust?

A spring passes below support and returns inside suspected accumulation. An upthrust passes above resistance and returns below it; a UTAD is that kind of test late in suspected distribution. Neither is mandatory, and the next reaction still matters.

Read the full explanation

SOS vs LPS

What are SOS and LPS?

A Sign of Strength, or SOS, is clearer upward progress in the developing range. A Last Point of Support, or LPS, is the later pullback or support test, often with a smaller price range and lower volume. SOS does not have to be the final exit from the range.

Read the full explanation

Open interest vs volume

How is open interest different from volume?

Volume counts contracts traded during a period. Open interest counts contracts still open. High trading volume does not necessarily mean more positions remain open.

Read the full explanation

High and low volume nodes

What are HVN and LVN?

High-volume nodes, or HVNs, have relatively large accumulated volume; low-volume nodes, or LVNs, have relatively little. They describe the selected profile. On a return, check whether price hesitates or passes through; neither distribution guarantees its next speed or direction.

Read the full explanation

Session vs composite profile

How is a session profile different from a composite profile?

A session profile covers one session. A composite profile combines a longer period, so the choice changes the scale and purpose of the analysis.

Read the full explanation

Initial Balance

What is Initial Balance in Market Profile?

Initial Balance is the high-to-low range during the profile’s selected opening period, often the first hour. Check the session start and included time blocks. Later movement beyond that range does not by itself predict the rest of the session.

Read the full explanation

Single prints

What are single prints?

Single prints are price rows represented by only one time block in a selected time-based profile, not by one trade. A later visit can remove that status. Detection rules and settings matter, and the area does not require a future return.

Read the full explanation

2. Interpreting market phases

Wyckoff method

What is the Wyckoff method based on?

It compares supply and demand, cause and effect, and effort versus result. Price and volume help test the development of a range. Volume does not reveal every holder’s position or guarantee the next direction.

Read the full explanation

Wyckoff market cycle

What are accumulation and distribution?

Accumulation describes position-building before an advance; distribution describes selling before a decline. The cycle also includes the rising markup and falling markdown phases.

Read the full explanation

Wyckoff accumulation

What does Arapov look for in accumulation?

The written checks look for a decline stopping, a range developing and tests of its lower area, followed by evidence of strength. A spring can be one test but is optional. Sideways movement alone does not confirm accumulation or a completed bottom.

Read the full explanation

Wyckoff on Forex and crypto

Can Wyckoff be used outside the stock market?

Arapov applies its price-and-volume ideas to other markets, but checks the data source. Forex and crypto volume have different coverage and reliability limits.

Read the full explanation

Composite Operator

Who is the Composite Operator?

It is a way of modelling the combined actions of large market participants. It does not mean one person controls the whole market.

Read the full explanation

Wyckoff phases A–E

What do accumulation phases A to E describe?

They describe the decline stopping, the range developing, a supply test, signs of strength and an advance beyond the range. Real markets need not follow the schematic exactly.

Read the full explanation

3. Understanding large participants

Smart Money

What does Smart Money mean?

Smart Money names large professional participants and their capital. Their purposes differ, and they can trade in opposite directions or lose money. Arapov compares activity with price progress and the next reaction rather than identifying a bank from one shape.

Read the full explanation

Large order execution

Why do large traders split their orders?

Buying or selling a large amount at once can move the price against them. Smaller orders can help build or close a position over time.

Read the full explanation

Smart Money and market phases

How does Arapov connect Smart Money with Wyckoff?

He uses accumulation, trending movement and distribution to explain how large participants build and close positions. Price and volume provide the observations for that interpretation.

Read the full explanation

Liquidity and manipulation

Does every market move mean manipulation?

No. Large trades can affect execution prices without deception. A false breakout does not prove spoofing, the placing of orders intended to mislead rather than execute. A permitted iceberg order that displays only part of its quantity is not automatically manipulation.

Read the full explanation

Bull trap vs bear trap

What are bull traps and bear traps?

A bull trap draws buyers into a move that fails; a bear trap draws sellers into a failed downward move. Arapov checks volume and the return through the level.

Read the full explanation

Large-player footprints

What clues does Arapov watch for large-player activity?

Arapov compares recorded trading quantity with how far price progressed and what happened next near an area. Heavy activity with limited progress can justify investigation, but does not identify the buyer or seller or prove a bank’s intentions.

Read the full explanation

SMC with volume

Why add volume to Smart Money Concepts?

Price zones can be marked in many ways after a move. Arapov adds exchange volume to check whether meaningful trading activity supports the interpretation.

Read the full explanation

BOS and CHoCH

What do BOS and CHoCH mean?

A break of structure, or BOS, usually passes a selected high or low in the existing direction. A change of character, or CHoCH, breaks against that sequence. Specify which turning points count and whether a candle must close beyond them; labels alone do not define entry.

Read the full explanation

SMC mistakes

What mistakes does Arapov warn about in SMC?

Do not enter from a marked zone or brief level crossing alone. Define the required reaction, the movement that would cancel the idea, the exit and trade quantity beforehand. If the exit distance is too large for the loss budget, reduce quantity or skip the trade.

Read the full explanation

Liquidity-grab entry

How does Arapov plan a trade after a liquidity grab?

The written scenario checks a failed break and return through the level, then the following reaction. The exit for a failed idea is planned beyond the failed break before entering. This is a conditional plan, not proof that every sweep reverses.

Read the full explanation

4. Where orders may gather

Liquidity pools

What is a liquidity pool on a trading chart?

In Smart Money Concepts (SMC), it means a price area where stops or other orders may be concentrated. It is a chart interpretation rather than a visible list of every order, and it is different from a DeFi liquidity pool.

Read the full explanation

Liquidity sweep

What is a liquidity sweep?

It describes price moving through a visible price boundary associated with possible orders. Check whether price returns through that boundary or continues beyond it; crossing alone does not establish a reversal.

Read the full explanation

Equal highs and lows

Where does Arapov look for liquidity zones?

The written checklist considers repeated highs and lows, session boundaries, daily or weekly extremes and round numbers. These are possible areas to check, not a verified universal personal routine; the following price reaction matters.

Read the full explanation

Sweep confirmation

Is a liquidity sweep enough to enter?

No. A return inside the old boundary still needs a subsequent reaction, such as a pullback holding the area and renewed movement. Before entry, define what would cancel the idea, the planned exit and the quantity that fits the permitted loss. A protective exit does not guarantee an exact fill.

Read the full explanation

Internal vs external liquidity

How is internal liquidity different from external liquidity?

External liquidity is beyond a range's outer highs or lows. Internal liquidity relates to smaller structures within the range.

Read the full explanation

5. Zones before strong moves

Order block

What is an order block?

A common SMC convention marks the last opposite-direction candle before a strong move through a selected high or low. It creates an area to test on a return, not proof that bank orders remain there. The convention is separate from the course’s general price-and-activity sequence.

Read the full explanation

Finding an order block

How does Arapov find an order block?

Choose the chart period and meaningful high or low first. Find the last opposite-direction candle before the move through that boundary, then fix which prices define its area. A smaller chart period can examine the return more closely; this does not establish a compulsory personal top-down order-block rule.

Read the full explanation

Order-block entry

Does Arapov enter as soon as price touches a block?

The course checks an area, trading activity and the next movement rather than entering from the first prominent candle alone. On a return to a block, assess whether movement against the intended trade stops and the intended direction resumes. Marking blocks is not a separate compulsory course rule.

Read the full explanation

Order-block mitigation

What does mitigation mean in SMC?

It describes a return to and reaction at a previously identified order block. A later return still needs fresh assessment rather than assuming the zone will hold.

Read the full explanation

Breaker block

What is a breaker block?

It is a former order block that has been broken and is considered in a new role. A former demand area may act as resistance, or supply as support.

Read the full explanation

6. Areas left by rapid moves

Imbalance vs FVG

How is an imbalance different from an FVG?

Imbalance can mean unmatched buying or selling quantity under a venue’s order rules. In SMC it often names rapid directional movement. An FVG is a particular three-candle non-overlap; its rectangle does not measure the exact difference between orders or prove an empty market.

Read the full explanation

Fair Value Gap

How do I recognise an FVG?

In a bullish FVG, the third candle's low is above the first candle's high. A bearish FVG reverses that relationship. The middle candle carries the strong move.

Read the full explanation

FVG pullback

Does price always return to an FVG?

No. The area can remain unvisited, and price can cross it without reversing. A marked gap does not guarantee later trading interest.

Read the full explanation

Order block and imbalance

How can an order block connect with an imbalance?

An order block uses a selected candle near a move’s starting area; an FVG uses non-overlap between the first and third candles. They can describe the same move and overlap, so they are not automatically two independent confirmations. Check the later reaction and trading activity.

Read the full explanation

FVG and volume

What makes an FVG worth watching in Arapov's approach?

The course checks a return toward an earlier breakout area, the trading activity and whether the expected direction resumes. It does not establish a compulsory FVG marking rule. If an FVG is present, it supplies a location to examine; its name cannot replace the later response.

Read the full explanation
