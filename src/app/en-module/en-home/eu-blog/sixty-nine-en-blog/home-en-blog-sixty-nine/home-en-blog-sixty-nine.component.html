1. Getting started with crypto

Cryptocurrency basics

What is cryptocurrency?

It is a digital asset whose transfers are recorded under a computer network’s rules. Its purpose, supply and holder rights depend on the particular asset. Buying it does not guarantee income.

Read the full explanation

Blockchain transactions

What is a blockchain?

It is a shared record maintained across a network of computers. Check transfer details carefully: a mistaken crypto transfer is generally not reversed like a bank payment.

Read the full explanation

Bitcoin, altcoins and stablecoins

How are the main types of cryptocurrency different?

Bitcoin has a limited supply; Ethereum supports applications. Other cryptocurrencies are commonly called altcoins, while stablecoins aim to track a reference such as a currency.

Read the full explanation

Crypto market risks

How is crypto trading different from other markets?

Many crypto markets trade around the clock on separate platforms, but service and withdrawals can still be interrupted. Check the exact product, the company serving the account and how the assets are stored; market access and custody are different questions.

Read the full explanation

Choosing a crypto exchange

What should I check before choosing a crypto exchange?

Check the company, country and service scope, purchase/sale/withdrawal charges, minimums and supported networks. Buying availability does not establish withdrawal availability. Exchange storage depends on the company; self-custody requires protecting and recovering your own access.

Read the full explanation

First crypto trade

What should I plan before buying crypto?

Choose the product and pair, quantity, entry condition, permitted money loss and exit instruction before ordering. Protective stops do not guarantee an exact sale price; a stop-limit can remain unfilled. Check actual fills rather than treating the intended price as the result.

Read the full explanation

Crypto chart analysis

How does Arapov analyse a crypto market?

The available course material does not establish a compulsory private crypto routine using issuance, halvings and Bitcoin dominance. Standard written checks here identify the exchange, product, chart interval and volume units, compare price with activity, and distinguish a capitalisation ratio from direct money flows.

Read the full explanation

Crypto demo trading

Why practise crypto trading on a demo account?

A demo account helps practise order entry and rules without losing real trading funds. The course treats unprepared movement to real money as a mistake. Demo execution and emotions can differ from live trading, and practice does not verify a platform’s ability to safeguard funds.

Read the full explanation

KYC checks

What does KYC mean?

KYC means Know Your Customer: a service checks a customer's identity. Passing identity checks does not, by itself, prove that an exchange is reliable.

2. Keeping keys and funds secure

Hot vs cold wallet

How are hot, cold and exchange wallets different?

A hot wallet uses keys in an online environment; cold storage keeps signing keys offline. A hardware wallet can keep keys inside a device. With an exchange account, the service generally controls the keys. These arrangements create different access and recovery responsibilities.

Read the full explanation

Hardware wallet

What does a hardware wallet protect?

A hardware wallet keeps its private key, the secret used to authorise transactions, inside the device while signing. It cannot make a harmful action safe. Check the transaction details and protect the recovery words that restore access.

Read the full explanation

Seed phrase

Why should I keep my seed phrase offline?

A seed phrase is a set of recovery words that can restore access to wallet accounts. Someone obtaining it may control the assets. Photos, cloud copies and entering it into an unsolicited site can expose it; use the wallet’s official recovery procedure.

Read the full explanation

Wallet backup

How should I prepare a wallet backup?

Record the recovery words accurately and keep separate protected copies. Use the wallet’s official backup-check procedure before relying on them, and verify any passphrase and compatibility requirements.

Read the full explanation

Exchange vs personal wallet

Should I keep all my crypto on an exchange?

An exchange controls its keys and withdrawal process, so a failure or suspension can affect your holdings. With a self-custodial wallet, you control the keys and must protect the access needed to recover them. Check the arrangement and its risks before depositing.

Read the full explanation

Multisignature wallet

What is a multisignature wallet?

A multisignature wallet uses multiple signing keys and a chosen number of required signatures, for example two out of three. Access can remain available after a lost key if enough other keys and the wallet’s configuration remain available.

Read the full explanation

3. Tokens linked to a reference price

Stablecoin peg

How does a stablecoin keep its price stable?

Different designs use reserves and redemption arrangements, assets committed to back issued tokens under program rules, or changes in supply and exchanges for a related asset. These mechanisms aim at a reference price, often one US dollar, but do not guarantee that it will hold.

Read the full explanation

USDT vs USDC vs DAI

How are USDT, USDC and DAI different?

USDT and USDC depend on their issuers and reserve arrangements. DAI uses software rules and assets committed to support those rules, which can include outside assets and issuers. Compare backing, redemption access and control rights rather than treating all three as interchangeable dollars.

Read the full explanation

Tether reserves

Why do USDT reserves matter?

Reserve assets help support the ability to redeem, but the report and redemption terms answer different questions. Check the reporting date, asset categories, scope, eligibility and possible suspension conditions. The available course evidence does not establish a personal USDT-reserve examination routine.

Read the full explanation

Stablecoin control risks

How do centralised and decentralised stablecoin risks differ?

A centralised design depends on its issuer’s backing and control rights. A protocol-based design depends on software rules and assets committed to support them, and may still rely on outside issuers. Neither removes all risk.

Read the full explanation

UST collapse

What did the UST collapse show?

UST lost its dollar-price link in 2022. Its failure shows that a stated reference price is not a guaranteed sale or redemption value. The relevant question is how that particular design is supported; it does not establish that every stablecoin has the same mechanism or that Arapov personally analysed that episode.

Read the full explanation

Frozen stablecoins

Can a stablecoin issuer freeze my tokens?

Some centralised tokens allow an issuer to freeze transfers at particular addresses. The possibility depends on the contract and the issuer's control rights.

Read the full explanation

USDT transfer network

Why must I choose the correct network when sending USDT?

The same token name can be used on different blockchains. The sender and receiving service must support the selected network, or funds may become inaccessible.

Read the full explanation

4. Crypto contracts and leverage

Perpetual futures

How is a perpetual contract different from a regular future?

A perpetual contract has no scheduled expiry date. A regular future has contract expiry terms. Neither contract gives you ownership of the underlying cryptocurrency.

Read the full explanation

Funding rate

What is a funding payment?

Funding is a payment between trades opened for a rise and trades opened for a fall, rather than simply an exchange fee. Who pays, the rate and timing depend on the contract. The payment direction can change.

Read the full explanation

Crypto leverage

How does leverage change gains and losses?

For the same contract quantity, leverage does not change the money gained or lost from the same price move. It reduces the funds required to open the position, so the result can be larger relative to those funds. Increasing the quantity increases monetary risk.

Read the full explanation

Liquidation price

Why does higher leverage bring liquidation closer?

If higher leverage lets you open the same position with fewer funds and you commit only that smaller amount, the same position has less room for an adverse move, other things being equal. The actual liquidation threshold also depends on maintenance and account rules; leverage alone does not fix it.

Read the full explanation

Liquidation cascade

What is a liquidation cascade?

Forced closures add orders to a moving market. Those orders can push price further and trigger more closures, amplifying the move.

Read the full explanation

5. Financial services on a blockchain

DeFi basics

What is DeFi?

DeFi provides financial operations through programs on a blockchain, a shared record of network transactions. You sign actions with a crypto wallet. Before committing funds, establish where the assets go and what conditions govern getting them back.

Read the full explanation

CeFi vs DeFi

How is CeFi different from DeFi?

CeFi relies on a company to manage the service and custody. DeFi uses blockchain applications and wallet interaction, changing who controls access and which risks you face.

Read the full explanation

DeFi building blocks

What services make up DeFi?

They include token exchanges, lending, stablecoins and data feeds called oracles. Connected services can pass a problem from one component to another.

Read the full explanation

DeFi swaps and pools

What can I do in DeFi?

Applications can offer token swaps, borrowing, lending and adding assets to pools used for other users’ swaps. High promised returns can involve high risks rather than a guaranteed income.

Read the full explanation

Smart-contract risk

Can an audited DeFi app still lose funds?

Yes. A code audit examines a particular version and can find weaknesses without guaranteeing their absence. Price data, linked assets, spending permissions and later changes can still cause losses. The available course explanation does not establish a private DeFi-app rule.

Read the full explanation

6. Supporting a Proof of Stake network

Crypto staking

What is staking?

Staking commits coins under a network’s rules to help confirm transactions in a Proof of Stake system. Rewards come with network, asset and service risks. It is not a guaranteed bank deposit.

Read the full explanation

Bitcoin vs Proof of Stake

Can Bitcoin be staked like Ethereum?

Bitcoin adds blocks through computational work, called Proof of Work, rather than native staking. Products advertised as Bitcoin staking use separate service or network arrangements; read what happens to the coins instead of assuming they participate in Bitcoin’s own block confirmation.

Read the full explanation

Validator vs delegation

How is running a validator different from delegating?

Running a validator means operating equipment and software that check network blocks. Delegation assigns coins to a validator without operating it yourself. A custodial service adds dependence on the company holding and managing the coins; the arrangements have different duties and risks.

Read the full explanation

Unbonding and slashing

What are unbonding and slashing?

Unbonding is leaving staking and can involve a wait or withdrawal queue. Slashing reduces committed funds for specified validator violations. An ordinary missed-duty penalty is not always slashing; both exit and penalty rules depend on the network.

Read the full explanation

Staking risks

Can staking rewards be outweighed by losses?

Yes. The coin's price can fall more than the rewards earned. A positive reward amount therefore does not by itself mean a positive total result.

Read the full explanation

7. Understanding token supply

Tokenomics

What is tokenomics?

It is a token’s economic design: issuance, distribution, uses and incentives. It helps explain which units are available, who may receive more and what a holder can do with the token. Those rules do not predict a future price by themselves.

Read the full explanation

Token supply and distribution

What should I check in a token's supply design?

Check the token’s purpose and holder rights, supply date and source, the supply figure used in fully diluted valuation (FDV), which applies the price to the provider’s broader supply figure, the next available portion and its recipient, and who can change the rules. Reconcile the documents with network records and the provider’s method before using the figures.

Read the full explanation

Token unlocks

What are vesting, cliffs and token unlocks?

Vesting is a schedule for releasing tokens; an unlock makes restricted tokens available. A cliff is an initial waiting period before tokens become available under the schedule. A first release may occur when that period ends.

Read the full explanation

Token design examples

Why compare Bitcoin, ETH and LUNA tokenomics?

They show different issuance mechanisms. Bitcoin’s scheduled new issuance declines toward a cap; ETH is issued as rewards while some fees destroy it. In the original Terra system, converting UST into LUNA destroyed UST and created LUNA. These rules explain supply differences, not a guaranteed price or a ranking.

Read the full explanation

Tokenomics warning signs

What tokenomics warning signs does Arapov watch for?

The available course does not establish a separate personal tokenomics filter. Unexplained issuance, unclear team releases, unknown powers to change rules and rewards without a stated source need investigation. They are unresolved risks, not automatic proof of fraud.

Read the full explanation

8. Trading price differences

Crypto arbitrage

What is crypto arbitrage?

It tries to profit from price differences across linked cryptocurrency trades. A gain exists only after the required trades finish and their costs are deducted.

Read the full explanation

Exchange vs triangular arbitrage

How is triangular arbitrage different from exchange arbitrage?

Exchange arbitrage compares prices across venues. Triangular arbitrage moves through three trading pairs, usually without transferring funds to another exchange.

Read the full explanation

Other arbitrage strategies

Are there types of arbitrage besides exchange price gaps?

A spot purchase paired with a futures trade opened for a fall can reduce directional price risk. Its result still depends on contract terms, changing periodic contract payments, price differences and costs. These conditions differ from a simple pair of spot trades.

Read the full explanation

P2P arbitrage

What risks come with P2P arbitrage?

It trades price differences in person-to-person markets. Problems with the other party, payment reversals or restrictions and account checks can outweigh the apparent gain.

Read the full explanation

Arbitrage costs

Why is arbitrage not risk-free profit?

The available quantity can disappear, orders can fill partly and separate exchanges do not execute their orders as one indivisible transaction. Fees and restoring balances reduce the result. Plan what to do if only one side fills before relying on the displayed difference.

Read the full explanation

Delta-neutral position

What does delta-neutral mean?

A delta-neutral combination aims to offset changes caused by a small move in the asset’s price, for example a coin purchase matched with a contract trade opened for a fall. Their prices, contract payments and execution can still differ, so it is not a fixed or risk-free return.

Read the full explanation

9. Recognising risks and scams

Types of crypto risk

What risks should a crypto beginner understand?

Price changes, service failures, loss of wallet access and fraud require different checks. Before committing funds, identify what you buy, who controls it and what permissions you grant.

Read the full explanation

Crypto drawdowns

Can a cryptocurrency lose most of its value?

Yes. A functioning project does not guarantee a profitable purchase. In the hypothetical unborrowed purchase, 100 coins bought for USD10 each are worth USD200 at USD2 per coin, an 80% decline before fees. Waiting does not guarantee recovery.

Read the full explanation

Crypto scams

What types of crypto scams should I recognise?

Examples include fake investment platforms, phishing messages that steal recovery access, recruitment-funded schemes and creators removing trading assets. Check verifiable actions, payment sources and permissions; a falling price alone does not prove fraud.

Read the full explanation

Pump and dump

How does a pump-and-dump scheme work?

Early holders build a position, promotion draws in buyers and the holders sell into that demand. Late buyers can be left with a falling price and few available buyers.

Read the full explanation

Scam warning signs

What should make me stop before sending funds?

Pressure to act, an unverified recipient, unclear withdrawal terms or requests for excessive wallet permissions are reasons to stop. Check the genuine site, legal entity, payment purpose and access before transferring.

Read the full explanation

10. Tokens driven by public attention

Memecoin basics

What is a memecoin in Arapov's approach?

A memecoin is a cryptocurrency associated with jokes, characters or online communities. Attention can increase demand but also fade. Issuance, creator powers and the ability to sell depend on the project; the name does not establish safety.

Read the full explanation

Memecoin liquidity

Why can a memecoin's price change so sharply?

With few available buyers or few assets available for trading in a pool, even a modest sale can receive much lower prices. Large holders can also sell; token-specific creator powers may change supply or restrict selling.

Read the full explanation

Memecoin supply and hype

How can supply and attention affect a memecoin?

A large supply does not ensure buying demand. If the reason for purchasing is simply that new buyers will arrive, the result depends on future interest rather than an established source of income.

Read the full explanation

Memecoin speculation

Do stories of huge memecoin profits show what most buyers earn?

No. Selected success stories do not describe all buyers or a complete sample. Costs and the prices actually available when selling matter; a displayed price is not proof that every holder could sell at that price.

Read the full explanation

Memecoin launchpad

What does a memecoin launchpad do?

A launchpad can automate token issuance and initial trading. That does not establish safety or future value. Check its distribution, fees, program-change and transition rules.

Read the full explanation

Bonding curve

What is a bonding curve?

It links a token's price with purchase or issuance parameters. In the model discussed, later purchases face a higher price, giving early and late buyers different entry conditions.

Read the full explanation
